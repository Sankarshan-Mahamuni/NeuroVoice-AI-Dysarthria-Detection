Plain-Language Medical Guide

What is Dysarthria? (Simplified)

Dysarthria is a motor speech disorder caused by neurological damage that impairs muscle control required for speech. The brain knows exactly what words it wants to say, and intellect is completely intact, but the muscles of the lips, tongue, vocal folds, or diaphragm are too weak, too slow, or uncoordinated to articulate sounds clearly.

>70% of stroke & Parkinson's patients develop dysarthria
Not Aphasia Muscle motor issue, NOT a language or thinking issue
Non-Invasive Detectable in <10s from microphone audio

Dysarthria vs. Aphasia: What is the Difference?

Dysarthria (Muscle Issue)

Speech muscles are weak or paralyzed. Thoughts and vocabulary are 100% intact, but words sound slurred, breathy, or strained.

Aphasia (Language Issue)

Brain's language center is injured. Muscle control is fine, but person struggles to find words, construct grammar, or comprehend speech.

⚠️ Sudden Onset? Think F.A.S.T.

If slurred speech appears suddenly, it is a medical emergency and may indicate an acute stroke. Check:

  • F - Face: Is one side of the face drooping?
  • A - Arms: Can the person raise both arms steadily?
  • S - Speech: Is speech slurred or strange?
  • T - Time: Call emergency services immediately!

Primary Neurological Causes:

Stroke / CVA Parkinson's Disease Amyotrophic Lateral Sclerosis (ALS) Traumatic Brain Injury (TBI) Cerebral Palsy Multiple Sclerosis (MS)

The 6 Clinical Subtypes of Dysarthria

Neurologists classify dysarthria by the specific area of the nervous system damaged. Each produces distinct acoustic patterns detected by our 144-biomarker engine:

Flaccid Dysarthria
Lower Motor Neuron Damage

Speech muscles lack muscle tone and are limp/weak.

  • Breathy, soft, exhausted vocal quality
  • Hypernasality (nasal leakage)
  • Audible inspiration / short breath cycles
Seen in: Bell's palsy, Myasthenia Gravis, Bulbar ALS
Spastic Dysarthria
Bilateral Upper Motor Neuron Damage

Speech muscles are excessively stiff, rigid, and hyperactive.

  • Harsh, strained-strangled vocal quality
  • Slow rate of speech with high effort
  • Monopitch and monoloudness
Seen in: Multiple strokes, Pseudobulbar palsy, Cerebral Palsy
Ataxic Dysarthria
Cerebellar Damage

Coordination, timing, and rhythm of speech muscles are broken.

  • Slurred, 'drunken-sounding' speech cadence
  • Irregular articulatory breakdowns
  • Sudden erratic loudness and pitch bursts
Seen in: Cerebellar stroke, Multiple Sclerosis, Friedreich's ataxia
Hypokinetic Dysarthria
Basal Ganglia (Dopamine Depletion)

Range and velocity of muscle movement are severely restricted.

  • Monotone, whispery quiet volume (hypophonia)
  • Sudden rapid rushes of mumbled words
  • Imprecise consonants with repeated syllables
Seen in: Parkinson's Disease, Progressive Supranuclear Palsy
Hyperkinetic Dysarthria
Basal Ganglia (Excess Movement)

Involuntary muscle spasms violently interrupt speech.

  • Unpredictable voice arrests and tremor
  • Sudden loud vocal blasts or prolonged pauses
  • Variable pitch breaks and grimaces
Seen in: Huntington's disease, Dystonia, Tourette's syndrome
Mixed Dysarthria
Multiple Neurological Systems Damaged

Combination of two or more subtypes as disease progresses.

  • Simultaneous muscle weakness and spastic stiffness
  • Rapid loss of intelligibility over time
  • Severe phonatory instability
Seen in: ALS (Amyotrophic Lateral Sclerosis), Advanced MS

144 Acoustic Biomarkers: How AI Listens

Instead of relying on human subjective impressions, our engine converts raw speech into 144 objective, quantitative acoustic features across frequency, velocity, and resonance domains:

🌊

MFCCs & Resonances (60 Features)

20 static Mel-Frequency Cepstral Coefficients plus mean, std, min, max. Captures vocal tract filter geometry, vowel formant positions, and tongue positioning.

Detects: Consonant slurring & tongue imprecision
⚡

Delta Dynamics / Velocity (40 Features)

First temporal derivatives ($\Delta$) tracking rate of change across consecutive frames. Measures articulatory speed and transition speed between syllables.

Detects: Speech bradykinesia & slow transitions
🚀

Delta-Delta / Acceleration (40 Features)

Second temporal derivatives ($\Delta\Delta$) measuring muscular acceleration, tremor, and motor jerkiness during phonation.

Detects: Ataxia, vocal tremors & motor dysregulation
📊

Spectral & Phonation Dynamics (4 Features)

Spectral Flatness, Spectral Centroid, RMS Energy, and Zero-Crossing Rate across full duration.

Detects: Breathiness, vocal fatigue & wheezing turbulence

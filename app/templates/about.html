1. Addressing Critical Methodological Flaws in Prior Research

Previous speech disorder detection literature has suffered from severe experimental shortcuts, including speaker identity memorization, test set leakage, and artificial feature corruption. Our clean-room framework addresses every prior limitation:

Experimental Dimension Flawed Common Practice NeuroVoice AI Clean-Room Architecture
Speaker Split Protocol Random utterance-level train/test splits (places same speaker in train and test, memorizing speaker identity). Strict Group Split by Speaker ID: Zero speaker overlap. Held-out participants are 100% unseen by all training and scaling steps.
Independent Subject Cohort Tiny cohorts (e.g. 15 subjects) claiming thousands of independent data points based purely on repeated word utterances. Multi-Subject Clinical Benchmark: 55 independent subjects across balanced healthy and pathological cohorts.
Acoustic Preprocessing Arbitrary 100-frame audio truncation (discards critical consonant transitions and internal pauses). Standardized 16 kHz Mono Pipeline: Energy-based framing across full utterance duration without silent head/tail clipping.
Feature Representation Naive concatenation of high-dimensional frozen embeddings with raw MFCCs without domain normalization. 144-Dimensional Multi-Domain Feature Vector: 20 MFCCs, first-order velocity ($\Delta$), second-order acceleration ($\Delta\Delta$), spectral centroid, flatness, and RMS dynamics.
Clinical Calibration Presenting raw softmax values as clinical confidence. Defensible Likelihood Scoring: Raw model scores are clearly presented as empirical classifier scores, not calibrated probabilities.

Scientific Justification of Synthetic Data Augmentation

Synthetic data generation must be scientifically grounded rather than arbitrary noise injection. We applied Within-Class Manifold Mixup combined with Acoustic Variance Perturbation:

\( \mathbf{x}_{\text{syn}} = \lambda \mathbf{x}_i + (1 - \lambda) \mathbf{x}_j + \epsilon, \quad \lambda \sim \text{Beta}(0.5, 0.5), \quad \epsilon \sim \mathcal{N}(0, 0.01 \boldsymbol{\sigma}_c) \)

This technique synthesizes realistic intermediate speaker physiology along the acoustic continuum within each diagnostic cohort, without crossing class boundaries or corrupting ground-truth clinical labels. On the held-out test set of 13 unseen speakers, synthetic augmentation boosted overall accuracy to 87.50% and dysarthria recall to 82.85%.

4. Mathematical Formulation of the 144 Acoustic Biomarkers

1. Mel-Frequency Cepstral Coefficients (MFCCs)

Log-energy outputs from 20 triangular Mel-scale filter banks after Discrete Cosine Transform (DCT):

\( c_n = \sum_{m=0}^{M-1} \log(S_m) \cos\left[ \frac{\pi n}{M} \left(m + \frac{1}{2}\right) \right] \)

Captures vocal tract shaping and glottal acoustic filter contours.

2. Velocity (\(\Delta\)) & Acceleration (\(\Delta\Delta\))

Polynomial regression window (\(N=2\)) computing articulatory velocity and acceleration:

\( d_t = \frac{\sum_{n=1}^N n (c_{t+n} - c_{t-n})}{2 \sum_{n=1}^N n^2} \)

Detects reduced articulatory speed, ataxia, and motor tremor.

3. Spectral Flatness (Wiener Entropy)

Ratio of the geometric mean to arithmetic mean of power spectral coefficients:

\( \text{Flatness} = \frac{\exp\left( \frac{1}{K} \sum_{k=0}^{K-1} \ln S(k) \right)}{\frac{1}{K} \sum_{k=0}^{K-1} S(k)} \)

Quantifies breathy vocal fold turbulence and friction noisiness.

4. Spectral Centroid & RMS Dynamics

Center of spectral mass and root-mean-square amplitude energy:

\( \text{Centroid} = \frac{\sum_{k} f(k) S(k)}{\sum_k S(k)}, \quad \text{RMS} = \sqrt{\frac{1}{T} \sum_{t=1}^T x(t)^2} \)

Identifies hypokinetic loudness decay and vocal pitch center shift.

5. Regulatory Compliance & Ethical Principles

Non-Diagnostic Screening Declaration

This software is distributed solely for academic research, algorithmic auditing, and proof-of-concept clinical workflow evaluations. In compliance with FDA Software as a Medical Device (SaMD) and EU Medical Device Regulation (MDR) guidelines:

  • This software must never be used as an independent diagnostic instrument.
  • Clinical diagnosis of dysarthria requires comprehensive multimodal examination by a licensed Speech-Language Pathologist, including oral-motor cranial nerve assessment, alternating motion rate (AMR) measurement, and perceptual Frenchay Dysarthria Assessment (FDA-2).
  • Model scores provide an acoustic screening indicator and are not calibrated probabilities.
